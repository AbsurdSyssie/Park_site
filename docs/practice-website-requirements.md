# Practice website requirements (England GMS)

Checked 17 November 2026 against the official England legislation text and NHS England guidance linked below. This is repository guidance for content preparation and review, not legal advice or a declaration that any particular practice complies. It applies the England GMS framework; it does not establish requirements for Welsh practices, or determine the terms of a particular PMS or APMS agreement. Check the practice’s actual contract and current national sources before recording a compliance decision.

## Source status and scope

The [GMS Regulations 2015, regulation 73](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2015/1862/regulation/73) sets the England online-presence duties. Regulation 78 covers the practice leaflet; [Schedule 3 Part 6 paragraph 48](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2015/1862/schedule/3/paragraph/48) lists its required information. On 17 November 2026, I retrieved and checked the complete paragraph 48 text from the official legislation XML response for Schedule 3, including every item (a)–(bb), against the checklist rows below. The XML identifies the revised text as effective from 15 June 2026. The current provision carries the paragraph 48(p) substitution made by the [2026 Amendment Regulations, SI 2026/532, regulation 1(2) and Schedule 1 paragraph 11](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2026/532/schedule/1/paragraph/11/made), in force from that date: it requires practice-premises opening hours and all means of contacting the contractor throughout core hours as required by Schedule 3 paragraph 4. The amendment instrument extends to England and Wales, but the underlying GMS Regulations and this guide’s GMS scope concern England; the legislation website’s “E+W” label is not a statement that this summary governs Welsh GMS practices.

NHS England lists a [Standard GMS contract for 2026/27, published 23 July 2026](https://www.england.nhs.uk/publication/standard-general-medical-services-gms-contract-26-27/). The detailed clause cross-references below are verified against NHS England’s [August 2025 standard GMS contract (2025/26)](https://www.england.nhs.uk/wp-content/uploads/2025/08/PRN02054i-standard-general-medical-services-contract-august-2025.pdf). Treat those contract clause numbers as the 2025/26 numbering; check the 2026/27 PDF and the practice’s executed contract before using them as current contract references. The statutory duties below are cited separately and are the baseline for this guide.

Do not apply this England GMS summary to a Wales practice. PMS agreements and APMS contracts can have different terms; NHS England publishes separate [standard contract documentation](https://www.england.nhs.uk/gp/investment/gp-contract/), and local agreements may vary. Identify the nation and contract route first.

## Online presence and review

Under regulation 73, an England GMS contractor must have a practice website or an online practice profile. A profile used for this purpose is on a website other than the NHS website, or a third-party online platform, and advertises the contractor’s primary medical services. A practice’s NHS website profile does not by itself meet this definition.

Publish all information required in the practice leaflet as ordinary web content on the site or qualifying profile. A PDF or other view/download of the leaflet alone does not meet this requirement. Review the online information at least once in every 12-month period, update it as needed after that review, and update it when a practice premises address, telephone number, displayed email address, or another stated appointment or repeat-prescription contact method changes. The annual contractual minimum is 12 months; a shorter local review interval may be a sensible project policy, but must be described as such rather than as the legal deadline.

Show prominent links on the homepage (or equivalent) to:

- the practice’s online consultation tool;
- NHS website symptom-checker and self-care information; and
- NHS England’s [General Practice Patient Guidance (“You and Your General Practice”)](https://www.england.nhs.uk/long-read/25-26-gp-contract-you-and-your-general-practice/).

Use destinations maintained by the relevant provider and check that links still work. Do not write or infer clinical advice to fill a link destination. In the 2025/26 standard GMS contract these links are clause 16.7E.5A and must be prominent under 16.7E.5B; regulation 73(5A)–(5B) states the statutory duty. The current project’s approved shared urgent-help panel placement is Home and Appointments only; this guidance does not change that decision or remove the standalone Urgent help page or footer link.

## Complaints information

The website should make the practice’s own complaints route easy to find and explain how patients can submit a complaint or comment using verified local contact details. The required leaflet item is paragraph 48(x); regulation 79 requires an England GMS contractor to establish and operate a complaints procedure complying with the NHS Complaints Regulations.

As a content review checklist, confirm that the page accurately explains:

- who handles complaints and how to contact the practice, including the local route for complaints about the practice;
- that a complaint may be made verbally or in writing, and how the practice records a verbal complaint;
- the usual 12-month period and the possibility of an extension where there is a good reason and a fair investigation remains possible;
- acknowledgement and an offer to discuss handling within 3 working days, and agreement about the expected response period (there is no single fixed response deadline);
- how the complainant will be kept informed if delayed, and that the investigation response is written and gives findings, any appropriate apology/action, and information about escalation;
- how a patient may complain to the service provider or the commissioner, and which commissioner is correct for the practice’s current location and service; and
- advocacy/support options and how someone may complain on another person’s behalf, including when consent or capacity needs consideration.

These process details are based on current [NHS England complaints guidance](https://www.england.nhs.uk/contact-us/feedback-and-complaints/complaint/). Let the complainant choose provider or commissioner; do not imply they can pursue both routes at once. Do not insert a made-up local response deadline, commissioner, email, form, named handler, consent rule or advocacy contact. NHS England’s national page says complaints about primary care services may go to the local ICB; use the current official ICB details for the practice, not an example from another area.

At the end of local resolution, provide accurate information about escalation to the [Public Service Ombudsman](https://www.ombudsman.org.uk/making-complaint/complaints-about-nhs-england/what-do-you-come-us) for NHS complaints in England. The Ombudsman may consider an unresolved complaint after the organisation’s final response; NHS England describes exceptions where the final response is overdue or the time limit to approach the Ombudsman is approaching. Check the Ombudsman’s current guidance before publishing detailed eligibility or time-limit wording.

## Accessibility, privacy and other published information

Keep accessibility standards and project test targets distinct. NHS England’s [Digital Service Manual accessibility guidance](https://service-manual.nhs.uk/accessibility) is updated for WCAG 2.2; it says NHS digital services should meet WCAG 2.2, and the [Accessible Information Standard implementation guidance](https://www.england.nhs.uk/long-read/accessible-information-standard-implementation-guidance/) describes WCAG 2.2 AA. The Park site-structure review names WCAG 2.0 criteria as its requested project baseline, not a conformance claim. Record outstanding manual and runtime checks; automated checks do not establish conformance. Determine the legal duties and applicability for the particular service rather than making an unsupported statement that one WCAG version applies to every GP practice website.

Other publication duties (for example accessibility statements, privacy information and CQC information) may arise under separate law, regulator rules, or NHS guidance. Confirm the current official source and applicability before describing a duty as contractual or statutory. Do not treat an existing page or an automated build passing as practice approval. In this project, practice facts, privacy/DPO review, clinical assurance and GP earnings/source review remain pending unless explicitly completed and recorded by the responsible practice reviewer.

## Repeatable content assurance

Use the [blank practice website review checklist](templates/practice-website-checklist.md) to record a review, and see the [completed Park Medical Centre audit](assurance/park-practice-website-audit.md) as a project-specific evidence example. The Park audit records observed implementation evidence and pending practice verification; it is not a reusable blank worksheet or a compliance sign-off.

For an initial review and after material updates:

1. Identify nation, contract route, applicable contract version, current regulations, and a responsible source owner for each practice fact.
2. Build a paragraph 48 coverage map. For every item, identify the patient-facing page/section and the evidence source; mark conditional items with the reason they apply or do not apply.
3. Check that all practice-leaflet topics appear as actual web content, and that the homepage prominently links to all three required destinations.
4. Check contact, premises, access, opening hours, staff, services, complaints, prescription routes and financial information against current authoritative practice records. No plausible guess is evidence.
5. Record the review date and reviewer. Set the next review no later than 12 months after this one; bring it forward after a listed contact change or another material fact change.
6. Run available deterministic checks for required pages/headings, internal links and external destinations. Then manually inspect rendered pages, mobile layout, keyboard and screen-reader behavior, link prominence, plain-language clarity, and accuracy against the source. Keep manual/runtime accessibility actions open until actually checked.
7. Record unresolved evidence, hazards, and practice decisions as pending. Do not claim legal compliance, clinical safety, DPO approval, practice sign-off or resolution of a hazard on the basis of this checklist alone.

For this Park project, keep the approved route map, existing public URLs and source content, six primary navigation labels/order, CQC footer widget, and the agreed shared urgent-help placement unchanged unless a later explicit project decision changes them. This is project-specific instruction, not a general England GMS requirement.
